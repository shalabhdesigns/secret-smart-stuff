# Platform, Policy and Cost Facts for the Faceless AI Shorts Channel

Research date: 2026-10-24. Sources are listed at the end, numbered [S1], [S2] and so on.

Most official pages (support.google.com, elevenlabs.io, openart.ai, docs.github.com) show **no publication date**. Where a page shows a date, it is given here. "UNVERIFIED" means the fact could not be confirmed on an official page.

## A. YouTube monetization and policy

### 1. YPP eligibility for Shorts

**Rules today (until 31 Jan 2027)** [S1]
- Full ad-revenue tier: 1,000 subscribers, plus one of these:
  - 4,000 public long-form watch hours in the last 12 months. Shorts-feed watch hours do not count.
  - **10 million qualified public Shorts views in the last 90 days.**
- Meeting the threshold does not guarantee entry. YouTube does a human review, which "typically takes about one month" [S1].

**Rules for new applicants from 1 Feb 2027** [S2 blog, dated 10 Aug 2026; S3 help page]
- 1,000 subscribers, plus one of these:
  - 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days.
  - **20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.**
- Channels already in YPP are not affected by the new entry bar.

**New monthly gate for Shorts revenue (from 1 Feb 2027)** [S2, S3, S4]
- A channel earns from the Shorts Creator Pool in a given month only if it has **10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days**.
- A channel below that stays in YPP and keeps long-form earnings. Shorts revenue restarts automatically once it is back above 10M.

**Activity rule for existing partners (from 1 Feb 2027)** [S3]
- A channel stays "active" if it meets any one of these:
  - 1,000 watch hours in 365 days
  - 1M Shorts views in 90 days
  - 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts uploaded every 90 days
- Partners must accept the updated modules in Studio by **31 Jan 2027**.

**Lower "fan funding" tier (unchanged in 2027)** [S3]
- 500 subscribers, plus 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3M Shorts views in 90 days.
- Third-party coverage also lists 3 public uploads in the last 90 days [S5, S6]. That upload requirement was **not** seen on the official page fetched.
- This tier unlocks memberships, Super Thanks/Chat, Shopping and Creator Partnerships. It does **not** unlock ad revenue.

**Other 2027 items** [S3]
- Targeted Shorts Ads: when an advertiser targets 5 or fewer channels, the creator gets a direct 45% share, on top of pool earnings.
- Premium Lite pool: 60% of net revenue.

### 2. How Shorts ad revenue sharing works [S4]
- Ads that run between Shorts in the feed are pooled each month, per country.
- **Music deduction:** Shorts with no music send all of their share to the Creator Pool. With 1 track, half goes to the pool. With 2 tracks, one third goes to the pool. The rest goes to music partners.
- A creator's allocation = their share of **engaged views** among monetizing creators in that country.
- **Creators keep 45% of their allocation**, whether or not music is used. The same 45% applies to Premium revenue on Shorts.
- Official example: $100,000 pool, 1 music track, 1% of engaged views → $900 allocated → **$405 to the creator**.
- Bot views and unoriginal re-uploads are not counted.
- Design implication: no-music or original/royalty-free audio avoids the pool split. YouTube Audio Library tracks get no Content ID claim [S8].

### 3. Typical Shorts RPM (ESTIMATES only; YouTube publishes no official RPMs)
- **AIR Media-Tech** (MCN; "data across thousands of channels"; published 23 Jan 2026, built on 2025 data) [S9]:
  - United States: ~$0.328 per 1,000 views
  - Switzerland: $0.205
  - New Zealand: $0.113
  - France: $0.102
  - Brazil: $0.045
  - Indonesia: $0.012
  - **India: ~$0.008 per 1,000 views**
- **Boss Wallah** (India; 23 Jul 2026; no data source named) [S10]:
  - Entertainment/lifestyle Shorts: ₹5–15 per 1,000 views
  - General niches: ₹5–30
  - Finance/business: ₹20–60
  - About ₹5,000–30,000 per 1M Shorts views
- No sources give a separate Hindi-vs-English RPM. The audience's country drives RPM, not the language. **Hindi-specific RPM: UNVERIFIED.**
- ainfluencer.com (Oct 2026) notes that published third-party estimates "disagree with each other by up to three times" [S11].
- Rough implication: 10M US-heavy views ≈ $3,000. 10M India-heavy views ≈ $80 (AIR) or ₹50k–300k (Boss Wallah). Both are estimates.

### 4. "Inauthentic content" policy and AI [S12, S13, S14, S15]
**Update history**
- 15 Jul 2025: "repetitious content" renamed to "inauthentic content". It now explicitly covers **mass-produced or repetitive** content. The reused-content policy did not change [S12].
- 16 Jul 2026: YouTube clarified YPP rules. Three types of channel can't monetize if they have "too much" of them [S13 TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026; S14 Android Headlines, 21 Jul 2026]:
  1. **Generic, repetitive or template-based content**, including content "easily made with AI, CGI, or templates" with little variation, and tutorials that just repeat common content.
  2. **Off-putting or distressing / emotionally manipulative content**, for example staged animal rescues. Channels built on this are removed from YPP, AI or not.
  3. **AI personas presenting as human experts** giving health, legal, financial or political advice. This is now on the official policy page [S12].
- The 2026 update was announced by Matt Halprin (VP Trust & Safety) on the Creator Insider channel. Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14Vm0CiyUVE (cited in [S13]).

**What gets a channel demonetized (official wording, summarized from [S12])**
- Similar or repetitive videos with low educational value, commentary or narrative, or minimal variation between videos.
- Characters repeatedly facing the same situation with the same outcome.
- Image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with minimal narrative, commentary or educational value.
- "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" that looks mass-produced and lacks the creator's own insight.
- Review is **channel-level**. One bad video is not the usual trigger. Mass-produced patterns are.

**What YouTube says is acceptable (quotes from [S12])**
- "using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented"
- "using AI to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual"
- With automated tools or templates, "the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision."
- The same intro and outro are fine if the bulk of each video differs. A series format is fine if each video has a distinct storyline or concept.

**Third-party claims (UNVERIFIED on official pages)**
- AIR Media-Tech (31 May 2026) lists overposting with no variation, AI voiceover over static images, and title-only template clones as triggers. It also mentions a "three strikes" removal rule [S15]. The three-strikes rule is **UNVERIFIED**.

### 5. Altered / synthetic content disclosure [S16, S17]
**When disclosure is required:** realistic content that would mislead viewers, such as:
- A real person appearing to say or do something they didn't.
- Altered footage of a real event or place.
- A realistic scene that didn't happen. Example: realistic AI weather hitting a real city.
- AI-generated music that is the main focus of the video.

**Not required for:**
- Clearly unrealistic content, such as fantasy or fully animated AI.
- Minor edits: colour filters, captions, upscaling.
- Script or idea help.
- **Cloning your own voice for voiceovers.**

**How to set it**
- **Studio:** upload flow → Attributes → "AI use" Yes/No. "Yes" adds a viewer label.
- **API:** `status.containsSyntheticMedia` (boolean) on `videos.insert` and `videos.update`. **Verified, it exists** (added 30 Oct 2024) [S17, S20].

**Consequences**
- Disclosure alone doesn't limit reach or monetization.
- YouTube may auto-label content using C2PA metadata or its own detection. Third-party coverage says it has done this for undisclosed photorealistic AI since May 2026 [S15].
- Repeated non-disclosure → penalties up to content removal or YPP suspension.

### 6. Shorts technical specs [S8, S18]
- **Max length: 3 minutes.** Square or vertical video up to 3:00 is classified as a Short (for uploads on or after 15 Oct 2024 on standard channels).
- 16:9 or wider is never a Short.
- **Aspect ratio:** vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) is standard. Square also qualifies. YouTube's page gives no specific resolution requirement, so **1080×1920 is the convention, UNVERIFIED as a requirement**.
- **API file limits:** max file size 256 GB; MIME types `video/*` or `application/octet-stream` [S18].
- **Classification:** there is no "Shorts" flag in the API. Classification is automatic from aspect ratio and duration. The `#Shorts` tag is not required (convention).
- **Copyright-claimed music on Shorts over 1 minute:** if a Short is longer than 1 minute and has an active Content ID claim, it is **blocked globally**. It can't be played, recommended or monetized until the claim is resolved. Shorts under 1 minute are not affected.
- Most Audio Library tracks can be used for up to 90 seconds in a Short. Some are limited to 60 or 30 seconds.

### 7. Official guidance on Shorts performance [S7, S19, S21, S22]
**View counting**
- **31 Mar 2025:** a Shorts "view" counts every start or replay, with no minimum watch time.
- The old metric was renamed **"engaged views"**. YPP eligibility and revenue use engaged/qualified views [S7, S21, S22].
- **24–27 Aug 2026:** "start-to-play" view counting was extended to all formats. Earnings are still based on "Engaged Shorts views" and "Engaged Watch Hours" [S7, S21].

**Studio metrics** [S19]
- **"Viewed vs. swiped away"** (also labelled "Stayed to watch"): the percentage of times viewers watched a Short versus swiped away.
- **"Shown in feed":** how often the Short appeared in the Shorts feed.
- Official advice: compare Shorts against your own typical performance and the same format, and look for patterns in top Shorts. Don't benchmark every Short against a viral outlier.
- YouTube does not officially say that "viewed vs swiped" is a ranking factor. That claim comes from third parties (e.g. Buffer [S23]).

## B. YouTube APIs

### 8. Data API v3 quota for videos.insert [S18, S20, S24, S25]
**Major change in 2025–2026**
- 4 Dec 2025: upload cost cut from ~1,600 units to ~100 units.
- **1 Jun 2026:** granular quota buckets introduced. `videos.insert` and `search.list` each have **their own bucket**.

**Today**
- `videos.insert` costs **1 unit in the "Video Uploads" bucket, default 100 calls/day**.
- `search.list` costs 1 unit in its own bucket, also 100/day.
- **All other methods share 10,000 units/day.** Read = 1 unit. Writes are typically 50.
- `videos.update` = 50, `thumbnails.set` = 50, `playlistItems.insert` = 50, `captions.insert` = 400, `videos.list` = 1 [S25].
- `videos.batchGetStats` (new, 3 Jun 2026) has its own bucket of 10,000/day.
- Pages last updated 2026-10-15.
- **More quota:** use the "YouTube API Services – Audit and Quota Extension Form". A compliance audit against the API ToS and Developer Policies is required first. There is no published timeline ("as soon as possible") [S24].

**Budget per Short**
- 1 upload + 1 thumbnail (50) + 1 playlist add (50) ≈ 100 units from the 10k pool, plus 1 upload call.
- So 100 uploads/day is the binding cap. That is far above what a channel needs.

### 9. Unverified API projects and "locked private"
**History**
- 28 Jul 2020: "All videos uploaded via the videos.insert endpoint from unverified API projects created after 28 July 2020 will be restricted to private viewing mode. To lift this restriction, each project must undergo an audit to verify compliance" [S20].
- Help Center: videos "locked as private due to upload via an unverified API service" **cannot be appealed**. You must re-upload via a verified service, and the developer can apply for an API audit [S26]. No date shown.

**CONFLICT, as of Oct 2026**
- The current `videos.insert` reference (updated 2026-10-15) says: **"Videos uploaded from unverified API projects are not restricted to private viewing mode."** The page also says default privacy is `public`, though some accounts default to `private` [S18].
- No revision-history entry announcing removal of the restriction was found [S20].
- A vendor blog (Postproxy, 6 Aug 2026) still says unverified apps can only upload private [S27].

**Status:** whether the private lock still applies is **UNVERIFIED / CONFLICTING**.
- Treat it as a risk.
- Test with one upload set to `public` or a `publishAt` schedule.
- If the video gets locked, file the Audit and Quota Extension Form. Typical requirements: describe the use case and show ToS/Developer Policy compliance.
- No official audit timeline is published. Timelines quoted by third parties are **UNVERIFIED**.

### 10. Scheduling and status fields [S17]
- `status.privacyStatus`: `private`, `public` or `unlisted`. Defaults to `public` if omitted.
- **`status.publishAt`** (ISO 8601):
  - Can only be set when `privacyStatus` is `private`.
  - On `videos.update`, you must also send `privacyStatus: private`.
  - Only works on a video that has never been published.
  - A past time publishes immediately.
  - Error `invalidPublishAt` (400) for a bad value.
- **`status.selfDeclaredMadeForKids`** (boolean): settable on insert/update. `status.madeForKids` is the resulting status. This is a COPPA decision, so set it to `false` explicitly for a general audience.
- **`status.containsSyntheticMedia`** (boolean): **exists**. Use it to disclose realistic altered/synthetic content.
- New: a `brandPartner` part on insert/update/list (7 Jul 2026) [S20].

### 11. OAuth scopes and refresh-token expiry [S18, S28, S29, S30]
**Scopes**
- Upload: `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload`. `youtube`, `youtube.force-ssl` and `youtubepartner` are also accepted.
- Thumbnails, playlists and metadata updates need `youtube` or `youtube.force-ssl` (upload scope alone is not enough for `videos.update`). This is standard practice; the exact per-method scope list was **not re-checked for every method**.
- Analytics:
  - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonly`
  - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics-monetary.readonly` for revenue. Needs a YPP channel; otherwise HTTP 403.
  - The reports.query page says requests "now require" `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly` [S29].

**Refresh-token expiry (critical for unattended runs)** [S30, updated 2026-10-16]
- **An app with External user type in "Testing" publishing status gets a refresh token that expires in 7 days.** Basic profile scopes are exempt; YouTube scopes are not.
- Tokens also die if:
  - unused for **6 months**
  - the user revokes access
  - time-based access expires
  - the limit of **100 refresh tokens per Google Account per client ID** is passed (the oldest is silently revoked)
- **Fix:** move the OAuth consent screen to "In production".
  - YouTube scopes are sensitive or restricted, so a public app shows an "unverified app" screen until Google verifies it [S28].
  - For a single-owner tool, an in-production but unverified app still works. The owner clicks through the warning once.
  - That this avoids the 7-day expiry follows from the rule's wording (it applies to "Testing" status) [S30]. Official text on the unverified-in-production case was **not found; UNVERIFIED**.

### 12. YouTube Analytics API for Shorts [S31, S32, S33, S34]
- **Available metrics:** `views`, **`engagedViews`** (added 26 Mar 2025, in all reports that have `views`), `averageViewDuration`, `averageViewPercentage`, `audienceWatchRatio`, `relativeRetentionPerformance`, `startedWatching`, `stoppedWatching`, plus likes, shares, subscribersGained and similar.
  - Thumbnail impressions/CTR: `video_thumbnail_impressions` and `video_thumbnail_impressions_ctr` were added in Reach reports on 15 Jan 2026 [S33].
- **Shorts filters:**
  - Dimension `creatorContentType` = `SHORTS`.
  - Traffic source `insightTrafficSourceType` = `SHORTS` ("swiping vertically from the previous video") [S32].
- **Audience retention:** the `elapsedVideoTimeRatio` dimension gives 100 points per video. No Shorts-specific limitation is documented [S32].
- **"Viewed vs swiped away" / "Stayed to watch":** **no matching API metric found** in the metrics reference [S31]. It appears to be Studio-only. **UNVERIFIED** that it is absent from the bulk Reporting API.
- **Data delay:** official docs (added 9 Sep 2026) say there is a **48–72 hour processing delay**. End dates are truncated to the last fully processed day. Use Data API `videos.list` for near-real-time view counts [S33].

### 15. GitHub Actions [S45, S46, S47]
- **Free plan, private repos:**
  - 2,000 minutes/month
  - 500 MB artifact/package storage
  - 10 GB cache per repo
- **Public repos:** free with standard GitHub-hosted runners. Self-hosted runners are free.
- Per-minute rates beyond the quota:
  - Linux 2-core $0.006
  - Windows $0.010
  - macOS $0.062
- **Max job time:** 6 hours per job on GitHub-hosted runners. Max workflow run time is 35 days. Free plan allows 20 concurrent jobs (5 macOS).
- **Scheduled workflows** (quotes) [S46]:
  - "can be delayed during periods of high loads". Common practice is to avoid minute :00 and schedule on odd minutes. That tip is not in the fetched quote, so it is UNVERIFIED as official guidance.
  - Shortest interval: every 5 minutes.
  - Runs only on the default branch.
  - "In a public repository, scheduled workflows are automatically disabled when no repository activity has occurred in 60 days." That page says this for **public** repos.
  - An optional IANA timezone is now supported.
- Third-party reports also say scheduled runs are sometimes dropped, not just delayed. **UNVERIFIED officially.** Add a watchdog, such as a second schedule or an external ping.
