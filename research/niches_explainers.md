# Niche Research: Explainer / Facts Shorts (faceless, AI-narrated)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: WebSearch + WebFetch of public pages only (Social Blade, vidIQ public stats, vling.net, OutlierKit, YouTube about pages, news). No logins.

## How to read this file

- **OBSERVED** = figure read on a page, with the source and the date the page showed. Social Blade pages = "SB", vidIQ = "vIQ", vling.net = "VL", OutlierKit = "OK".
- **ESTIMATE** = my own inference or calculation, or a third-party *modelled* number (for example vidIQ revenue).
- vling "Join Date" sometimes means the date vling indexed the channel, not the YouTube creation date. Where Social Blade gives a creation date, I use that.
- Most stats pages **do not say whether a video is a Short**. When I call a channel "Shorts-heavy", that is an ESTIMATE based on video count, titles and emojis, unless stated otherwise.
- Social Blade often showed "--" for daily subscriber gains. Daily *view* gains were more reliable.
- Pages that did not load: playboard.co search (client error); Social Blade for @megaprojects, @thebplan, @lesics, @halfasinteresting, @etymologynerd1, @anemap, @factflixhindi (handle form); OutlierKit for @historyofsimplethings. vIQ pages for History on Maps, Geographics and @etymologynerd loaded without stats. The WebSearch budget (shared limit) ran out near the end, so Hindi coverage for niches 2–4 is thinner than I wanted.

## Cross-cutting context (applies to all niches)

### YouTube "inauthentic content" policy
- **OBSERVED:** On 2025-07-15, YouTube updated its YPP monetisation rules. "Repetitious content" was renamed **"inauthentic content"**. It targets mass-produced, templated, low-variation uploads, including AI channels with generic voiceovers and visuals (Gulf News; SubSub blog).
- **OBSERVED:** AI tools are allowed, but creators must disclose realistic synthetic content, and videos must show significant original creative input (SubSub blog). Storyboard18 reported YouTube's framing as "no new rules, just clearer language".
- **Implication (ESTIMATE):** The biggest risk is not "AI narrator". It is a niche whose videos all look the same (same template, same voice cadence, swapped noun). A niche with *varied visual explanations* (maps that change, diagrams that differ per topic) is safer than "list of 10 facts over stock images".

### Shorts RPM
- **OBSERVED (AIR Media-Tech, updated 2026-01-23):** average Shorts RPM in 2025 was **US $0.328 per 1,000 views** and **India $0.008 per 1,000 views**.
- **OBSERVED (OutlierKit, updated 2026-09-13):** gross CPM by niche. Travel & Geography $3–10. History $5–12. Business case studies $10–25. AI & Tech $8–20.
- **Implication (ESTIMATE):** Shorts ad revenue alone is small. A Hindi-only audience earns almost nothing from Shorts ads. Real money needs (a) an English, Western-heavy audience, (b) long-form spin-offs, and/or (c) sponsors and affiliates.

### India map rules
- **OBSERVED (Scroll.in, 2022-12-31):** Union MoS IT Rajeev Chandrasekhar warned companies to "use correct map of India if you want to continue working". Wrong depiction "can lead to a police case". Earlier precedents: a MeitY notice to Twitter (Nov 2020) and a request to Wikipedia (Dec 2020).
- **Implication:** Any map of South Asia must show India's official boundaries. That includes J&K, Ladakh, PoK, Aksai Chin and Arunachal. The same map may then upset Pakistani or Chinese viewers. It is a lasting hazard for niche 1 and partly for niche 5.

### Newcomer evidence
**The strongest of all five niches.** History of Simple Things went from 0 to 1.04M subs in ~2.7 years (created Feb 2024). Simple Things – Surprising Histories went from 0 to ~296K subs in ~13 months, with multiple 2–7M-view videos in its first 5 months. Many 2026 copycats have under 500 subs, which shows that execution matters.

### Recurring formats and hooks
- "Why do [object]s have [odd feature]?" (pliers' serrated jaws, jeans' small pocket, stones on railway tracks). This is the dominant winning hook.
- "What [label] REALLY means" (expiration dates).
- "How is X made?" and "The rise and fall of [product]".
- Brand-founder "poor boy → billions" arcs (long-form).

### Content gaps (ESTIMATE)
- Object-design "why" questions about Indian or Asian everyday things (pressure-cooker whistle, steel tiffin, auto-rickshaw meter) in English. They are unusual to Western viewers and familiar to the diaspora.
- Higher-craft visual explanations: animated cutaway diagrams instead of stock clips.
- Transport and infrastructure objects overlap with niche 3, which allows a hybrid.

### Trademark risk
Naming a brand in a factual history is generally OK (nominative use). Risks come from (a) brand logos and packaging used as thumbnails, (b) implied endorsement, and (c) AI recreations of logos. Mitigation: focus on *generic objects* rather than brands, show no logos, and use generic AI renders. Keep brand stories to a minority of the output.

**Recommendation (ESTIMATE / judgement):** rank **Origin stories of everyday things** first. Lean toward the object-design "Why does X have Y?" sub-format rather than brand stories. It has the clearest proof that a 2024–25 faceless newcomer can reach 300K–1M subs through Shorts. It is advertiser-safe and evergreen, and it suits AI object renders plus programmatic cutaway diagrams. To set the channel apart from History of Simple Things and its copycats:
1. A consistent diagram/cutaway visual signature. Varied visuals per video also reduce inauthentic-content risk.
2. A lower, quality-first cadence (1/day, not 4/day).
3. A sub-lane of Indian and Asian everyday objects explained for a global English audience.
4. Borrowing transport and infrastructure objects from niche 3 ("why railway tracks have stones" was a 3.4M-view hit) as a natural bridge.

Keep geography as a strong secondary lane only if India-boundary map rules are built into the template.
