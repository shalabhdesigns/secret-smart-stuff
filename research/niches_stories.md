# Niche Research: Story-Driven Faceless AI Shorts

Research date: 2026-10-24. Tools: WebSearch + WebFetch only.

## How to read this file

- **OBSERVED** = a number I read on a public page. The source and date are given.
- **ESTIMATE** = my own calculation or judgment. It is not a published figure.
- Stats pages used: Social Blade (SB), vidIQ stats pages (vidIQ), OutlierKit (OK), vling.net (VL), creatordb (CDB), qoruz (QZ), Playboard (PB), SponsorRadar (SR).
- Many SB "videos" tabs need a login. OK, Playboard and some vidIQ pages failed for some channels with client errors. These are noted where they happened.
- YouTube channel pages render only the description through WebFetch. They show no counts and no video lists.
- **The search budget ran out near the end.** The folklore and what-if sections have fewer newcomer checks than I wanted. More searches can be run in a follow-up.
- "Shorts vs long-form" is often **not verifiable** from these pages. I say so where it matters.

**What this means:** every niche here carries "template channel" risk. The things that defend a channel are:
- a consistent, named narrator persona
- an original script angle
- visible research or sources
- varied structure, not one fill-in-the-blank template
- the owner approving each video, which helps.

### Newcomer evidence
- **Yes, moderate.** Relax History AI: 0 to 117K in ~21 months (OBSERVED). Its growth has slowed to ~1K subs a month.
- Crumb Lore: 34K in ~5 months, with a narrow "food history" angle (OBSERVED).

### Formats and hooks that work
- "The ENTIRE history of X"
- "The shortest/longest/strangest X ever"
- "What happened to…"
- "Filthy/WEIRD secrets of…"
- One forgotten object or food that "changed the world" (Crumb Lore)

### Content gaps
- Narrow sub-angles still win: food, objects, jobs, everyday items.
- Indian and South Asian history told in English, or Hinglish, *without* politics. Hindi channels that win lean political, so there is room for apolitical "strange India" stories.
- A recognisable narrator voice. Most AI history channels are anonymous and generic.

### Newcomer evidence
- **Weak.** I found no 2024–2026 English mystery Shorts channel past ~10K with healthy growth.
- The one with traction (Unsolved Files) got it from a sensational real-person case, and then stalled.
- Several 2026 AI channels with 150–200 uploads sit at 15–2.2K subs. That is direct evidence that **volume alone fails here**.

### Formats and hooks that work
- "They vanished. The ship didn't." (best video on a failed channel: 6× its median, but tiny)
- Recap-style "Mystery Recapped"
- Countdowns ("5 disappearances never solved")

### Content gaps
- **Historical, non-violent mysteries**: lost ships, lighthouse keepers, lost cities, unexplained objects and signals, vanished expeditions over 100 years old. This avoids real victims and living families.
- Indian mystery incumbents in Hindi are dormant (2.7–2.9M subs, tiny current views). A fresh, well-made Hindi or Hinglish mystery channel faces weak *active* competition. But incumbents mix in religion and spirituality, which is excluded.

### Advertiser limits
- Tragedy, real victims, graphic description and "shocking" framing bring limited ads.
- The Jan 2026 update helps only if the content is dramatized and non-graphic.
- Recent real cases with living people bring defamation and misinformation risk ("FBI releases…" style titles).

### Newcomer evidence
- **Strong, but in an adjacent sub-format.** Bizarro! went from 0 to 1.22M in 10 months (OBSERVED, Spanish). Its creepy "if you see this, run" urban-legend Shorts show that the legend and creature-warning hook can still explode for a newcomer.

### Where it becomes religion-adjacent (avoid or handle carefully)
- Angels, demons, Lucifer, Hindu, Greek or Norse deities as *gods*, Buddha, Ravana "proof".
- Black magic (kaala jaadu), exorcism, spirits tied to living faiths.
- Safer: secular creatures (Wendigo-type cryptids need cultural care), trickster tales, haunted places, local legends, sea monsters, cursed objects, urban legends.

### Content gaps
- **Cozy, non-horror folklore** for broad audiences.
- Indian regional *lok kathas* (non-deity: talking animals, clever villagers, haunted forts) told in English or Hinglish.
- African, SE Asian and Slavic creatures. Newcomers exist here but are weak on quality and hooks.
- Original **creature design** can become brand IP.
