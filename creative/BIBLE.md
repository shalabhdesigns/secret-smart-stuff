# "It's Fine." — show bible (draft v1, 25 Oct 2026)

## The one-line idea
Dev says "It's fine." Nothing is fine.

A cartoon sketch show about the tiny daily disasters everyone pretends are fine: bad haircuts,
group chats, bosses, mums, landlords, splitting the bill. Every viewer has said "it's fine" while
dying inside. That is the shared feeling the show is built on.

## Format
- **Length:** 20–40 s vertical Shorts. Long-form later: 8–12 min "episodes" that join 3–4 sketches
  with a thin storyline, plus compilations.
- **Structure of every sketch:**
  1. **Cold open on the problem (0–2 s).** No set-up talk. We see the disaster first.
  2. **Escalation.** Dev's people-pleasing makes it worse, usually in 2–3 steps.
  3. **The spiral (optional, about 3 s).** A cut inside Dev's head, shot as a different genre:
     thriller, horror, courtroom, nature documentary, Bollywood song, heist. This is the show's
     signature. It lets us play any genre while staying one show.
  4. **The button.** The punchline, then Dev's "It's fine." with his eye twitch.
  5. **The loop.** The last line or image leads naturally back into the opening.
- **Language:** English with Indian accents and settings, worldwide in feel. The jokes must work
  without knowing India; Indian flavour is seasoning, not the joke.
- **Tone:** warm, clean, advertiser-friendly.
  - No swearing.
  - No real people or brands.
  - Nobody is the butt of the joke for who they are. The joke is always the situation and Dev's
    over-politeness.

## Main character: DEV
- **Who:** 28, junior project coordinator at a mid-size office. Shares a city flat with Meera.
  Originally from a small town; his mum calls him every day.
- **Look (lock these):**
  - Big round head, slim and slightly hunched.
  - Warm brown skin.
  - Messy black hair with **one stubborn cowlick** that always survives, even the haircut.
  - **Oversized round black glasses**, thick expressive eyebrows.
  - **Mustard-yellow hoodie** over a light-blue shirt, blue office lanyard.
  - Grey chinos, white sneakers a size too big.
- **Personality:** kind, funny in his head, and a lifelong **people-pleaser**. He cannot say no,
  cannot complain, cannot send food back. He over-thinks every social moment.
- **Wants:** to be liked, and one day to be brave.
- **Flaw:** avoids conflict at any cost, which always costs more.
- **Strength:** hopeless optimism. He tries again tomorrow.
- **Signature mannerisms:**
  - "It's fine." with a **left-eye twitch**.
  - A too-wide frozen smile.
  - A nervous double thumbs-up.
  - Swallowing loudly before any hard sentence.
- **Speech:** polite, over-apologetic, trailing off ("Could you maybe... no, it's fine...").
- **Comic engine:** the gap between what Dev thinks (shown in the spiral) and what he says out loud.

**Rules:**
- A sketch has 2–3 characters at most.
- Dev is in every sketch.
- Each supporting character has one clear comic job. If a character doesn't serve the joke,
  cut them.

## Visual style
- **Look:** stylised 3D cartoon with chunky proportions (big heads, small bodies), big expressive
  eyes, soft clay-like shading, warm saturated colours, and soft "animated feature" lighting.
- **Original designs only.** We never copy existing characters.
- **Animation approach ("limited but alive"):**
  - Dialogue shots are lip-synced from a single character frame (ByteDance OmniHuman 1.5), which
    produces mouth, eyes, brows, head and shoulder movement from the voice track.
  - Action and reaction beats (snips, falls, flashes) are short image-to-video clips.
  - Backgrounds stay still.
  - Cuts land on the beat of the joke.
- **Consistency:** every frame is generated from the **character sheet** (references below) plus
  a locked description. A new set (shop, office, flat) is generated once, then reused as a
  reference.
- **Sound:**
  - One voice per character, fixed.
  - Music only under the spiral and the button.
  - Sound effects only where they *are* the joke: snip, phone shutter, record scratch, ominous drone.
  - Silence before the punchline.

## The sketch engine (for 365+ ideas)
Every idea = **an everyday arena** × **a social pressure** × **Dev's people-pleasing move** ×
**a twist**.

- **Arenas** (40+), for example:
  - barber, dentist, office, group chat, wedding, gym, flight, Uber;
  - landlord, restaurant, family WhatsApp, parents' video call, job interview;
  - first date, birthday party, roommate, IKEA-style flat-pack, elevator, queue;
  - delivery app, customer care line, neighbour, doctor's waiting room, school reunion;
  - and so on.
- **Pressures:** politeness, guilt, FOMO, awkward silence, hierarchy, peer pressure, being seen,
  being helpful.
- **Dev's moves:** says yes, over-explains, fake-laughs, lies to be nice, apologises for nothing,
  agrees to "just one more".
- **Twists:**
  - escalation to absurdity;
  - a role reversal (Dev finally says no, at the worst moment);
  - a callback;
  - a fourth-wall glance;
  - a spiral genre swap;
  - Meera's one-line truth.
- **Series formats:**
  - "Dev vs. ___" (one service a week).
  - "Mummy calls" (a recurring 15-s mini).
  - "Office Mondays".
  - "Things that are fine" (montage).
  - Holiday specials (Diwali, Christmas, New Year).
  - Occasional 3-part mini-arcs ("Appraisal Week").

## Idea scorecard (before anything is made)
Each item is scored 1–5:
- Relatability ("this is literally me")
- Shareability (who would you send it to?)
- Clarity in 2 seconds
- Punchline strength
- Fit for a recurring character
- Visual gag potential
- Production cost (fewer characters and sets = cheaper)
- Brand safety

Only ideas scoring ≥ 28/40 get made.
