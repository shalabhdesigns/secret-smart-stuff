### Facts and sources
- Seaweed goes soft quickly when it touches rice; konbini onigiri can sit on the shelf for hours, so the packaging keeps seaweed and rice separate until opening. — https://explorejapaninsider.com/how-to-open-onigiri-japan/
- Convenience store rice balls purposely separate the rice and seaweed so the seaweed does not lose its crispness. — https://matcha-jp.com/en/732
- A layer of film between the rice and seaweed prevents the seaweed from getting moist and sticking to the rice. — https://apeachyplate.com/japanese-onigiri-rice-triangles/
- The film is designed to be removed when eating, and keeps the nori crunchy. — https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01982/
- Seven-Eleven Japan launched packaged onigiri in 1978 (nippon.com: onigiri packed in special film separating rice from nori; Food in Japan: first packaged onigiri line in 1978). — https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01982/, https://www.foodinjapan.org/japan/convenience-store-onigiri/
- Tab 1 is pulled down from the top and around the onigiri. Tabs 2 and 3 then slide the film out from each half, leaving the seaweed wrapped around the rice.
