# Audit of the production template (v2 → v3), 27 Oct 2026

Scope: the show bible, craft docs, playbook, and the code that writes, stages, renders, checks and learns
(`shorts/sketch/*`). Evidence: the owner's notes, the episode records in the state DB, the rendered versions, and
the research in `docs/research/` (R1 comedy theory, R2 LLM humour and judging, R3 animation/editing/sound/captions,
R4 platforms and analytics, R5 screenwriting and Indian context).

## What the evidence says about v2
- **Ep 2 needed 9 versions across two premises.** The first premise (barber) was dropped. The replacement went to 7
  versions, through 5 owner fix notes, and cost $5.25. It is still not approved.
- **Both Ep 2 premises are stock meme situations.** They were Diwali "kabaad/polythene" hoarding and soan-papdi re-gifting.
  This matches the research: LLM comedy converges on the same tropes (R2: Jentzsch & Kersting; Wenger & Kenett).
- **The owner reported "first word cut off" three times.** The writer model "fixed" it twice in the script, by adding a
  silent shot and then a sacrificial "Arey". The real causes were in the audio code:
  - the line trimmer cut into soft first sounds;
  - speech started at 0.03 s, and players swallow the first fraction of a second.
- **The owner was told "Gupta Aunty is now drawn" when her drawing had failed.** The review message repeated the model's
  intent, not what actually happened.
- **The staging was almost static.** People stood facing camera while the dialogue talked about objects (a soan papdi box,
  a remote) that were never shown, or were shown wrong (the same blue card for every object).

## Weaknesses, in priority order (what changed)

| # | weakness (v2) | evidence | v3 change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The writer judged itself.** The "audience panel" and table read used the same model that wrote the scripts, with absolute 1–10 scores. | Self-preference, position and verbosity bias; LLM raters overrate LLM creativity vs experts (R2: Panickssery; Zheng; Wang; Ismayilzada) | An independent judge (a different model): blind, both orders, evidence before scores, anchored 1–5 rubric, pairwise only between finalists (Tripathi). LLM judging is a coarse filter only. Owner decisions and real data are the final word, and the judge is calibrated against them (`judge.py`, `perf.py`). |
| 2 | **Ideation started from topics.** This drifted to clichés and repeated meme situations. | Both Ep 2 premises are known memes; LLM sameness (R2) | Observation-first stage: 24 specific behaviours, each with an unspoken rule, at high temperature, from rotated life areas. Adds a cliché bank, a repetition filter, and a "fresh angle" field (`room.py`, `creative/cliches.json`). |
| 3 | **No comedy mechanics at joke level.** Beats existed, but no reveal word, no A/B meaning and no planted callback. | Incongruity-resolution and script opposition; repetition-shift; callbacks/fluency (R1: Israel; Raskin; Loewenstein & Heath; Topolinski) | Joke map with A/B meanings and reveal word; pattern-pattern-shift; callback planted in the first 10–20 s; "unfunny test" (R2: Horvitz) (`WRITER_CARD.md` §4). |
| 4 | **Premises ignored why a violation is funny.** | Benign violation, psychological distance (R1: McGraw & Warren 2010; McGraw et al. 2012) | Premise card names the violation and why it stays benign; severity × distance rule. |
| 5 | **Unclear relationships and stiff dialogue.** Owner: "who is the girl to Dev?", "not organic", fragments such as "kitne sweet". | Owner notes; sitcom dialogue uses vocatives and emotion and trims vagueness (R5: Quaglio); 200 ms turn gaps (Levinson & Torreira); intention/obstacle (Mamet, Sorkin) | Who-is-who cue required by 5 s; complete-sentence rule; stock-phrase list; distinct voices with sample lines in the bible; dialogue rubric anchor; owner lessons (`gates.py`, `SHOW_BIBLE.md`). |
| 6 | **Art was requested after the script.** Failures were "written around" invisibly, and the owner saw people missing. | Gupta Aunty and dev_box both failed; v6/v7 claims were false | Art for the chosen premise is drawn before scripting (stage 2b). Characters are generated by editing an approved figure (keeps style and framing). Pose edits keep the approved head and legs. Failures are planned around by design (door, phone) and reported truthfully (`art.py`, `flow.art_for_premise`, `flow.diff_summary`). |
| 7 | **The storyboard didn't check that the picture matched the words.** | Blue-card bug; phone voice with nobody holding a phone; owner: "where is the soan papdi dibba?" | Poses list their props. Storyboard gate: props, presence, hearing, continuity, beat labels, loop mirror (`gates.storyboard`, `judge.storyboard_check`). |
| 8 | **The loop was only a wish.** | Owner: "doesn't loop back seamlessly"; loop craft = identical frames + seam audio (R3) | Loop bridge in the script. The last shot must mirror the first. The music bed crossfades into its own start. The seam is checked on the finished video (`gates`, `sketch_assemble`, `qa.loop_seam`). |
| 9 | **Audio defects** | First word clipped (×3); voice-only character "almost unhearable" | Trimmer keeps 120 ms before soft onsets; first line at 0.30 s; phone/off-screen voices level-matched (+1–2 dB); transcript check that the first word is heard (`prep_lines.py`, `director.py`, `sketch_assemble.py`, `qa.transcript`). |
| 10 | **Revisions rewrote the story.** Owner: "why write an entirely new story instead of fixing the same one?" | Owner note | Revision protocol: same story, smallest complete change, re-checked against hard checks and red flags. The review message states the real diff. |
| 11 | **Length was forced to 35–45 s by speeding voices and cutting lines.** | Owner: "Don't cut off dialogue to fit 45 seconds"; length research conflicts (R4) | Target ~55 s (48–60). Tempo is fixed; a weaker line is cut instead. A 35 s vs 60 s block test is planned. |
| 12 | **No learning loop.** Raw notes were pasted into prompts, and no analytics fed back. | The same complaint repeated three times | Notes are distilled into standing lessons with counts (`lessons.py`). Retention is mapped per beat and fed to the writer (`perf.py`). Experiments run as pre-registered blocks (`PILOT.md`). |
| 13 | **Weak or duplicated sources, and conflicting docs.** | 35–45 s vs "never over 60"; Rocky-centric structures; blog sources | One canonical template (`TEMPLATE.md`). Two operative cards (writer, staging). Superseded docs are archived. Primary sources are verified with labels (`research/`). |
| 14 | **Cost and time.** About 9 large model calls per episode with no caching, and fix loops. | $5.25 for Ep 2 | Prompt caching (system prompt reused at ~10% of the price). Cheap stages filter before expensive ones. "No episode today" beats sending a weak one. |

## Kept from v2 (it worked)
Character designs and the puppet engine; the expression and acting rules (`EXPRESSIONS.md`); world memory and fixed
voices; Telegram approve/fix/drop; private upload with a schedule; metadata rules; the publish slot.

## Known limits (not solved)
- **Single model family.** Only Anthropic models are available here, so the judge is a different model of the same
  family. Same-family bias can remain (R2: Verga). Mitigations: anchors, blind order swaps, owner calibration, and real
  audience data.
- **Unproven decisions.** No research proves the effect of loops, the ideal pause length, or the ideal Shorts length for
  Hindi comedy. These are hypotheses to test (`PILOT.md`).
- **Limited puppet library.** It is still small (few poses per character, 3 sets). Art is generated on demand within the
  $5/week cap; complex physical comedy (falls, chases) remains out of reach.
- **Transcript check language.** It assumes the speech-to-text engine handles Hindi well. Its results are reported, not
  blindly trusted.
