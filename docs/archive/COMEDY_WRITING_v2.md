# Comedy writing for Sab Theek Hai (read every episode)

Owner's standing rule: every episode must be **genuinely laugh-out-loud funny, deeply relatable to ordinary Indian
viewers, and made to be shared and rewatched**. A video that only fills the schedule is a failure. Story first:
characters and places serve the story, never the other way round.

## 1. Where the laugh comes from
- **Recognition ("yeh toh mere ghar mein hota hai").** The strongest Indian sketch comedy works because viewers see
  their own mother, father, relatives, boss, friend or themselves. Start from a real, specific moment almost everyone
  has lived: mummy's "5 minute" call that lasts an hour, the relative asking marks and salary, the family WhatsApp
  group forward, the landlord's rules, the "beta, ek chhota sa kaam", splitting a bill, the delivery guy calling
  from the wrong lane, the boss's "quick call", saving the good bedsheet for guests, the "dabba" that must be
  returned full, Diwali safai, shaadi invitations, results day, the AC remote, "data khatam".
- **Specific beats general.** "Mummy asked about my life" is nothing. "Mummy called to ask if I ate, then asked
  what I ate, then asked why not dal" is a laugh. Real names of things, real phrases, real numbers.
- **Truth said out loud.** The funniest line usually says what everyone thinks but nobody says (Dev's inner voice,
  Meera's deadpan).
- **Benign violation.** Something goes wrong in a way that is safe to laugh at: social rules, small lies, pride,
  family logic. Never punch down (no jokes about caste, religion, region as an insult, body, poverty, disability).

## 2. The shape of a 35-45 s sketch
1. **Hook at 0 s:** the first line already contains the conflict or a strange specific ("Mummy ka video call aaya
   hai... camera on karke"). No greetings, no setup scene.
2. **Game:** one unusual pattern that drives everything ("every answer Dev gives creates a bigger problem").
3. **Heighten three times**, each beat bigger and each a *different* lever (more specific, more emotional, higher
   stakes). Rule of three: pattern, pattern, break.
4. **Twist / reversal** the audience didn't see coming but that feels inevitable afterwards (status flip, hidden
   info, the "victim" was in control all along).
5. **Punch, then a button**: a last 1-3 s tag that recontextualises the whole thing or loops back to the first line
   (rewatch trigger).

## 3. Lines
- 3-10 words. Funny word LAST (Hindi is verb-final: restructure or split into a tag line).
- Real people interrupt, trail off, answer a question with a question, repeat themselves when stressed.
- Office/tech words in English, feelings in Hindi. Light UP flavour, never stage Hindi. Nobody explains the joke.
- Characters want something in every line. Comedy = a want blocked by a character trait.

## 4. Shareability and rewatch
- **Share trigger:** a viewer must think of a specific person ("isko bhejta hoon") - a mother, a flatmate, a
  boss, a friend who always does this. Write that person into the sketch.
- **Rewatch trigger:** a button that changes the meaning of the first line, a detail planted early and paid off,
  or a final line so quotable people comment it.
- **Comment trigger:** a relatable question the audience wants to answer ("aapki mummy bhi aisa karti hain?") -
  goes in the description/pinned comment, never in the dialogue.
- Timely beats help: festivals, seasons, cricket, results, wedding season, salary day, month-end. Don't date-stamp
  so hard that the episode is useless next month.

## 5. The world
- The show's world grows: new family members, colleagues, neighbours, places. Use whoever the story needs.
- Reuse established people and places when they fit (continuity rewards loyal viewers), never force them.
- Rocky and the barber shop only for stories that are really about them.

## 6. Kill list (never)
- Premises about nothing ("Dev is bored"), jokes that need explanation, random noises or faces instead of a joke,
  catchphrases forced in, lecture or moral at the end, the same situation as a recent episode.
