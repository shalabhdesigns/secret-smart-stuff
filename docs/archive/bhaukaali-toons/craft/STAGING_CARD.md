# Staging card: storyboard, acting, edit, sound, captions, loop (read by the stager and the checks)

Numbers marked [H] are starting values we tune with retention data. Evidence: docs/research/R1, R3, R4.

## Storyboard (the plan JSON is our animatic)
- Every shot has a **beat** label (hook/setup/escalate1-3/turn/punch/button) so retention can be read per beat later.
- **Show what the words are about.** If a line is about an object, the object is visible in the right hands (pose props),
  or the staging makes clear it is off-screen. Never show the wrong prop. "The camera can do the explaining" [E Mamet].
- **Presence**: anyone physically present is on screen; voice-only people are on a phone (someone on screen holds a phone)
  or clearly elsewhere (next room, behind the door). Nobody answers a line they couldn't hear.
- **Continuity**: consecutive shots of the same moment keep pose, props and screen side (180° rule); inner-voice
  close-ups match the pose of the surrounding scene.
- Staging = "presenting an idea so that it is unmistakably clear" [E Thomas & Johnston/Lasseter]: one idea per shot.

## Shots and cuts
- Open on a character mid-situation; the first line starts at 0.30 s (players swallow the first fraction of a second —
  owner heard the first word clipped three times). No title card, no fade-in.
- A wide shot early (by shot 2–3) so geography reads; wides for physical gags, close-ups for reactions and punches
  (close-ups go with more laughter [F, preprint]).
- Cut priority: emotion > story > rhythm > eye-trace > screen direction [E Murch]. Cut to the reaction after the line;
  a reaction shot borrows emotion from the shot before it (Kuleshov effect, real but modest [F Barratt; Calbi]).
- Dialogue shots ~1.5–4 s; silent reaction shots 0.6–1.2 s; punch reaction 1.0–2.0 s. Never 3 same-size shots in a row.
- Inner voice: Dev only, ≤3. Black beat: ≤1, 0.4–0.8 s, never first or last.

## Acting
- One idea per face; expression changes once per change of thought (every ~2–4 s), listeners less than speakers
  (docs/craft/EXPRESSIONS.md).
- Takes: anticipation 4–6 frames → accent → settle ~7 frames; double take for delayed realisation [E School of Motion].
- **Before the punch: stillness.** Characters stop moving and the music drops; pause 0.5 s [H; pauses are longer before
  high-surprise lines in stand-up, F correlational, Ma et al.; a null result exists, Attardo & Pickering].
- A genuine smile reaches the eyes; a mouth-only smile reads fake — use it for Dev's forced smile on purpose [F FACS].

## Sound
- Dialogue first. Music ≥10 LU below speech (≤15) and ducked under speech; ambience ≥15 LU below [F Torcoli et al.].
- Music drops ~0.4 s before the main punch; sound effects only for a visible action, ≤3 per video. No decorative dings.
- Voice-only characters (phone, next room) are as loud and clear as on-screen ones (owner lesson).
- Master −14 to −15 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.

## Captions (most viewers watch sound-off in public [F Verizon/Publicis 2019])
- Always burned in. ≤2 lines, ≤42 characters per line [S Netflix]; break after punctuation, never split a phrase.
- Reading speed ≤17 characters/second target, 20 maximum [S Netflix adult 20 / children 17].
- In-time with the voice (±2 frames), out ~0.5 s after it, never across a cut [S Netflix]. Never show a punch word before
  it is spoken. Voice-only speakers get their name prefix. Easy-to-read type is funnier [F Topolinski].

## Loop (no evidence that loops raise replays — we do it for the owner and test it [H])
- The last shot repeats the first shot's set, size and cast; the last line leads into the first line (a question the
  first line answers, an action that restarts, or the same word) — the plan states the **bridge**.
- No freeze, fade or dead air at the end; the music bed crossfades into its own start; captions never straddle the seam.
