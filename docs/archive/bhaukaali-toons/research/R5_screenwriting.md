# R5 — Short-form story, dialogue, Hinglish, Indian sketch context (verified 27 Oct 2026)

Labels: [F] research finding · [E] expert/practitioner opinion · [H] hypothesis (ours).

- Story Spine (Once upon a time / Every day / But one day / Because of that ×3 / Until finally / And ever since then)
  — invented by Kenn Adams (late 1980s, improv); reached Pixar via teacher Rebecca Stockley. [E]
  NPR interview with Kenn Adams (S. Detrow, 2026): https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619
- Emma Coats' "22 rules" are one story artist's 2011 tweets, not an official Pixar method. [E]
  A. Amidi, Cartoon Brew, 19 Oct 2013: https://www.cartoonbrew.com/ideas-commentary/must-read-commentary-on-pixar-joe-ranft-and-the-22-rules-of-storytelling-90047.html
- UCB: base reality → first unusual thing → game → heightening ("if this is true, what else is true?"). [E]
  Besser, Roberts & Walsh, *UCB Comedy Improvisation Manual* (2013), https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/859530405 ;
  summary: https://graemewrites.substack.com/p/anatomy-of-a-scene
- Helitzer's THREES (Target, Hostility, Realism, Exaggeration, Emotion, Surprise); "most good jokes state a bitter truth". [E]
  https://bakadesuyo.com/2013/11/how-to-be-funny/
- Status is something you *do*; comedians lower status (Johnstone, *Impro*). [E] https://jamesclear.com/book-summaries/impro
- Mamet memo to *The Unit* writers: "Who wants what? What happens if they don't get it? Why now?"; no "as you know"
  exposition; "the camera can do the explaining". [E] https://www.slashfilm.com/a-letter-from-david-mamet-to-the-writers-of-the-unit/
- Sorkin: intention and obstacle; "if you don't have that, it's journalism". [E]
  https://www.masterclass.com/classes/aaron-sorkin-teaches-screenwriting/chapters/intention-obstacle-11ba8c15-7856-490d-85bb-eb0601e02c55
- Turn-taking: modal gap between speakers ≈200 ms; 30–40% of transitions have brief overlap (~100 ms). [F]
  Levinson & Torreira, *Frontiers in Psychology* 2015: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full
- Sitcom (*Friends*) dialogue vs natural talk: broadly similar, but less vague language, more emotional language,
  more vocatives (names), less variation. [F, one show] Quaglio 2009, https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/scl.36 ;
  review https://linguistlist.org/issues/21/239/
- Hindi–English code-mixed humour corpus (3,453 tweets; κ=0.821); humour often depends on trending-event context. [F]
  Khandelwal et al., LREC 2018: https://aclanthology.org/L18-1193
- Humorous tweets switch language slightly more (7.84 vs 6.50 switches) — tiny effect (r≈0.04). [F, weak]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02295
- Indian digital comedy craft (journalism/interviews) [E]:
  TVF — middle-class relatability, real places, lived experience, avoid stock types: https://theprint.in/ground-reports/youtube-favourite-to-ott-darling-tvfs-success-has-even-bollywood-calling/1876714/
  Gullak (Durgesh Singh) — first-hand domestic issues: https://thebetterindia.com/281712/gullak-writer-durgesh-singh-season-3-sonyliv-small-town-success
  Gullak panel — "not a show about the ten per cent": https://indiantelevision.com/news-headline/vidnet-2022-why-do-narratives-like-gullak-work-on-ott-220425/
  FilterCopy — relatable situations, open on action not establishing shots, data picks ideas: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/enterprise/big-idea/quick-short-videos-can-mint-fortunes-simply-let-user-data-lead-the-script-5886
  BB Ki Vines — characters drawn from real relatives: https://youthink.sxccal.edu/images/Interview%20Answers%20-%20Mr.%20Bhuvan%20Bam.docx.pdf

  clear in the video): https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939 ; BNS s.299 (ex-IPC 295A, secondary source):
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_295A_of_the_Indian_Penal_Code ; India's Got Latent outcome (IFF, Sep 2026):
